## Summary
Slice 0 adds the first public items to `grdr-ports` and `grdr-ports-testkit`: the `Clock` and `Entropy` ports, the `grdr-model` instant type `Monotonic`, the one retry executor `grdr_ports::retry` with the `RetryState` it works on, their deterministic reference adapters (`ManualClock`, `SeededEntropy` and `block_on`), and the conformance runner that every later port suite extends. Layers 0 and 1 take `now` and `entropy` as arguments and do no I/O, and only the programs' OS adapters read a system clock or the system generator. Every retry bound comes from a `limits.toml` retry table through RFC 0007's `RetryLimits`.

Until #1959 is decided the executor has exactly two callers: the outbound calls the uplink's own tasks start, and an adapter's connection set-up inside its constructor, before the adapter accepts any call. The uplink keeps one `RetryState` for its origin. An adapter's set-up state lives only inside its constructor; the adapter keeps a copy of its reading, shown only as the set-up's result. A state owns its clock and its entropy. Each operation class has its own policy, retry budget, design/22 section 2.7 attempt counter, breaker and health call, on its own route. A failed attempt counts against the peer only when its registered code says the peer did not serve it, or when it got no answer before its cutoff. A class's breaker opens only when its health call also fails, and the health call follows the route of the calls it stands for. A probe with no verdict at its deadline counts as no answer. Against a dead peer each class makes about one call per backoff.

No instance that a request handler reaches builds a `RetryState` until #1959 is decided, and no call a request starts, local or remote, runs through one. Nothing here names a tenant context, a tenant witness, the seal, the mint authority, a fence or a storage port; those stay in RFC 0002, which amends this RFC's runner for its storage suites. Both crates enter `surfaces.toml` as `experimental`.

## Motivation
#281 (Clock, Entropy, the reference adapters and the runner) and #283 (the retry executor) are the first code in both crates, and the first runnable path needs them (#645, comment 7074036495): the daemon links `grdr-ports` for `Clock`, `Entropy` and the executor. RFC 0002 sections 3 and 4 stated this surface. They share no mechanism with that RFC's tenant contexts, seal, mint authority or fences, which are what RFC 0002's review is about. A public crate changes only by RFC (D-61; design/30 section 3), and `experimental` sets the break rules, not the route (the ruling on #1535). If nothing changes, #281 and #283 wait on a tenant boundary they do not use.

Rounds 1 to 3 showed that a shared retry state needs more than a shared counter, and revision 4 removed the inputs a client had into it. Rounds 4 and 5 then found every remaining major in machinery slice 0 does not need: calls detached from outside requests, states that serve many principals, and the daemon's decide path. The session moved that machinery to #1959 and kept the executor slice 0's first runnable target uses (#1924 comment 7079671821). Round 6 then found that the kept scope still let request handlers reach the executor, through the embedded server and the endpoint's stores, and that one breaker shared by two classes on two routes could not end a failure on one route. Revision 7 narrows the callers to two and gives each class its own breaker (#1924 comment 7080286990). Round 7 found that a probe with no verdict was lost when another caller took the next permit, that a set-up state kept by its adapter showed a reading that never changed, that the lint's module list could not tell a constructor from a call path, and that the classes still coupled through their shared budget. Revision 8 counts such a probe as no answer, ends the set-up state with its constructor, lists constructors by function, gives each class its own budget and gives Degraded a code (#1924 comment 7080898013).

## Design

### 1. Scope and the split from RFC 0002
This RFC states RFC 0002 sections 3 and 4, and the parts of RFC 0002's Rules 1, 2, 7, 8 and 12 and sections 5, 12 and 14 that bear on them. RFC 0002 keeps everything else. From revision 10 it has no sections 3 and 4, points here, and amends section 5's runner (section 8).

The rules of section 2 and the failure modes of section 6 gather what RFC 0002, #281 and #283 already say. Otherwise the text follows the source, except in nine places:
1. The runner (section 5) is generic over its subject. The source bound it to a storage `Fixture`, so the Clock suite, which is generic over `A: Clock`, could not run on it. A case returns its `Outcome`, and the runner's types are `#[non_exhaustive]` or have private fields, so RFC 0002 adds case kinds, fixtures, stop points, induced faults and `NotApplicable` by amendment.
2. "The RFC of #1710" becomes #1850 for the `grdr-model` retry types and RFC 0007 for `RetryLimits` and `Coded`, as RFC 0007 section 7 asks.
3. `Monotonic` gains `from_nanos` and its derives. Without a constructor, neither an OS clock nor a test can make the instant `ManualClock::new` takes, and `monotonic() >= d` needs the order.
4. Before each call, the executor checks the breaker, then the budget, then takes the permit, in one step under the state's lock, so a refused call spends nothing.
5. One `RetryState` serves every call it is built for (R9). It owns its clock and entropy. Each class has its own policy, budget, attempt counter, breaker and health call. The source let one run at a time borrow a state with no counter.
6. The executor has exactly two callers until #1959 is decided, a set-up state lives only inside its constructor, and a deadline comes from a fixed `limits.toml` row (R11). The source took any caller and any deadline.
7. A failure counts against the peer only when its registered class and Connect code say the peer did not serve the call, and only if the peer served no call meanwhile (R12). The source counted every failure.
8. A class's breaker opens only when the class's health call confirms the failures, and its probe after an open period is that health call, which follows the route of the calls it stands for (R13). After `breaker_failures` unhealthy probes in a row the next probes are the probing runs' own calls, which close the breaker only on `Done`, for at most one cap. An abandoned probe frees its slot and the class's next probe waits one call timeout, and a probe with no verdict at its deadline counts as no answer at the next permit. Under #282 R9 a dropped probe held the breaker half-open for good.
9. A class's breaker opens for at most the counter's ceiling and the class's cap, and its health call and probes are timed by the class's call timeout. The source timed it by the step's delay, so a hint set how long every caller was refused.

The split needs items 1 and 2. Items 3 to 9 each fix a defect of the source that would stop #281 or #283, or leave a peer unprotected. Where the source is silent this RFC states that `start` is the call's own start; that a deadline past the maximum instant is the maximum instant, as #282 has it for the breaker; that the runner passes one seeded entropy to its cases in turn; that `RetryError` is exhaustive; that each call and each failed attempt reads the clock once; the late reports on an open breaker; the derives of every public type; how `to_toml` writes a string; and the values of the Clock suite's case parameters.

What moved to #1959. Revisions 4 and 5 grew retries for callers slice 0 does not have. Every remaining major of rounds 5 and 6 sat in them, and each needs design that design/07, design/20 and design/22 do not settle yet. #1959 takes them with every case and requirement of rounds 1 to 6:
- calls detached from an outside request, with `run_for`, `ClientWait` and `ClientWatch`, and the per-target bound on detached calls, which has to follow design/20's admission classes;
- states that serve many principals, with the shared budget and counter between principals, a state per destination for webhook and export peers, and the call probe against a principal; `Serves`, the parameter that would declare whom a state serves, comes back with them;
- the daemon's decide path and the verdict-provider fan-out of design/07, with provider suspension;
- every instance a request handler reaches: the ports of the embedded single-organisation server, the endpoint's local stores and the spool the decide path writes (revision 7).

Until #1959 is decided, those ports return their errors to their callers unretried (R11).

### 2. Rules
- R1. **Dependencies.** `grdr-ports` depends on `grdr-model` and `grdr-schema` and on no other crate, third-party crates included. The privileged daemon links it, so whatever it links is in the daemon's closure. `grdr-ports-testkit` depends on `grdr-ports`, through the listed same-layer edge, on layer 0 and 1 crates of the workspace, and on no third-party crate; RFC 0002 adds the Arrow crates for its Events reference adapter. It is never in the normal-dependency closure of a layer 4 program, resolved with all of that program's features for each target triple the release ships. A program's `Clock` is `grdr-os-clock`'s and its `Entropy` is `grdr-crypto`'s `OsRandom`, the system generator (#1927 R11; design/03 ARCH-003). So neither `ManualClock`, which whoever holds it can move, nor `SeededEntropy`, whose every later output one output reveals, reaches product code.
- R2. **Async shape.** `Clock` has `Send + Sync` as supertraits. `sleep_until` is declared as returning `impl Future<Output = ()> + Send`, which an implementation may write as an `async fn`. There is no `async-trait`, no boxed future and no `futures` crate, so `Clock` is not dyn-compatible and a caller is generic over `C: Clock`. `Health` has the same shape, and `Arc<H>` implements it for every `H: Health`, so a test keeps a handle on the health call it gave a state. `Entropy` is synchronous and stays dyn-compatible, because `Engine::step` takes `&mut dyn Entropy` (design/03 section 3). `RetryState<C, R, H>` is `Send + Sync` when `R: Send` and `H: Health`, so the future of `run` is `Send` while it borrows a shared state. A WebAssembly host passes `now` and an `Entropy` to the engine as values and implements no `Clock`.
- R3. **Open traits, confined implementations.** `Clock`, `Entropy` and `Health` are not sealed. Outside test code, `Clock` is implemented only in `grdr-os-clock` and `grdr-ports-testkit`, and `Entropy` only in the `random` module of `grdr-crypto` and `grdr-ports-testkit`. Each list is ARCH-003 data (section 7), and R1 keeps the kit out of every program. `Health` is implemented once per class of the uplink and by each adapter that sets up a connection to a peer of its own, under R13, and by the kit's double. An adapter that only calls other ports builds no state. RFC 0002's seal covers its storage ports only.
- R4. **Layers 0 and 1.** Layers 0 and 1 hold no `Clock`, read no clock or randomness source and do no I/O (spine section 5; D-05). A layer 1 function that needs time takes `now: Timestamp` or a `Monotonic` as an argument, and one that needs randomness takes an `Entropy`, as `Engine::step` does. `Monotonic` and `Entropy` live in `grdr-model`, and `Monotonic` is a value that reads no clock. `Clock` lives in layer 2, so no layer 0 or 1 crate can name it (ARCH-001). Above layer 1, design/03's ARCH-003 makes `grdr-os-clock` the only crate that calls a system clock and the `random` module of `grdr-crypto` the only reader of the system generator, so nothing in `grdr-ports` or `grdr-ports-testkit` reads either (design/03 section 10, Time and Randomness).
- R5. **Bounds are parameters.** Every bound the executor applies comes from the `RetryPolicy` of the state's classes: attempts, elapsed time, delays, call timeouts, budget, breaker threshold, open time, probe and health-call timeout, quiet period, the wait after an abandoned probe, the unhealthy probes before a call probe, the length of a call-probe turn, and the failed attempts a budget window needs before `failure_share()` reads above 0. The unhealthy probes and those failed attempts are the breaker threshold again, on purpose: the breaker trusts as many unhealthy health calls in a row as it trusts failed calls before it acts, a share counts only once as many attempts have failed, and no second threshold needs a row. A turn lasts at most the class's cap, the longest the breaker opens. The share above which the uplink raises Degraded is the uplink's own bound, the `limits.toml` row `uplink.degraded-failure-share` in `permille`, 500 by default (section 8). The executor reads no threshold of health. Product code builds a policy only with `RetryPolicy::from_limits`, from a `[retry.<name>]` table of `limits.toml` or a narrowing of one (RFC 0007 sections 5 and 6; #1850). A `run_until` deadline is the state's clock plus a `limits.toml` value (R11). `block_on`'s poll bound is an argument. No type here holds a tunable bound (ARCH-019). The Clock suite's case parameters (section 3) are part of each case's definition, not bounds the product applies. They are named constants in one file, `crates/grdr-ports-testkit/src/clock/params.rs`, which ARCH-019 exempts by its full path, and a change to one is a new kit version and an amendment (section 8).
- R6. **Errors.** `RetryError<E>` has no code. Its caller maps every variant to a code of the subsystem that failed, and the caller's rule class decides what the failure means (D-15). It is exhaustive, so a variant added by amendment fails every caller's build until the caller maps it. `for_class` and `set_policy` cannot fail, because each class has its own budget. The kit's errors, `ClockError` and `Stalled`, have no code and hold no free text. All three are entries of the ARCH-017 exemption list in `architecture.toml`, each with its reason, so the lint reads data and special-cases no name (section 8). The executor has one code of its own, for a cutoff: `GRDR-RETRY-0001` `ATTEMPT_CUT`, `retryable` with `deadline_exceeded`, which `last_code()` names after an attempt cut at its cutoff (section 4). No `RetryError` carries it; Degraded does. The kit's `ScriptedFault` does carry codes, because `Health::Error` and every operation error are `Coded`: one registered code per class and Connect pairing that section 7's cases need, in the kit's own area `KIT`, `GRDR-KIT-0001` to `GRDR-KIT-0007` (section 4). #283's tests use them, so no test needs a code of another area, and `architecture.toml` confines them to the kit and test code. Each type implements `LogSafe` (#1850): `RetryError<E>` where `E` does, with the `Monotonic` and the give-up reason it carries. No type here holds a person-facing string.
- R7. **Data classes and assurance.** `Monotonic`, `Timestamp`, attempt counts, the attempt counter, delays, deadlines, the breaker's state, the abandoned count, the health reading and its code, the failure share, a `ClassReading`, seeds, reports and the kit's errors are `structural` (P8). Entropy output is never logged or stored. The executor never logs `E`, which keeps the class its own type declares. Assurance effect: none; no action class changes `visibility`, `control`, `on_failure` or `tamper` (P1).
- R8. **Scale.** The executor adds no request of its own beyond the health call (P15). One call makes at most `free_retries_ceiling + 1` retries in its class's budget window, as RFC 0007 section 5 bounds it, and a class's retries spend only its own budget. The health call runs at most once per confirmation and once per open period, however many callers wait on the class. While a class's breaker is closed its health call runs at most once per health latency plus the quiet period of one class call timeout. A call probe takes the place of a health probe and adds no call. Once a class's breaker has opened, the class reaches a peer that fails every call once per open period. With design/22 section 2.7's two uplink classes (control at a 120 s cap, data at 15 minutes), each with its own breaker that opens after 5 failures, one device against a dead origin makes 86.8 calls an hour on average, 74 to 92 in single runs, health calls included: about 241,000 requests a second at ten million devices. One breaker shared by both classes made 58 to 71 in revisions 4 to 6, about 161,000 to 197,000 a second, but could not end a failure on one class's route (section 1). Most of the figure is design/22's own control cap: a mean open period of 60 s is 60 probes an hour per device, about 167,000 a second. Each further class on an origin adds about 3,600 / (cap / 2) calls an hour per device, its cap in seconds. Counting attempts per run made about 1,460 an hour, about 4 million a second (the revision 8 bench, posted on #1924). An origin that accepts every call and fails it while its health call passes costs about 114 calls an hour per device, the counter's backoff alone (M6 in section 7).
- R9. **One state per caller instance.** Until #1959 is decided a `RetryState` is built in two places only, and every call it is built for, from any task, uses it:
  - the uplink's origin (`grdr-uplink`, design/22 section 2.7), for the outbound calls the uplink's own tasks start. Its control and data classes are one state: the second is built by `for_class` with its own policy and health call. The classes share the clock and entropy, and each keeps its own budget, attempt counter, breaker and health call on its own route. design/22's counter and cap per class hold; its budget per uplink and its one breaker per origin each become one per class (section 8). The calls a local request starts do not use the state: Enrol for `grdr join` (design/22 section 4.2) and the ApprovalRequest a STEP_UP routed to a remote approver sends (design/18 section 4). Each is sent once and its error goes back to the request that started it. The ApprovalRequest goes out after its Decide has returned the deny (design/18 section 4), so its error goes to the pending approval the retry token names, and the next Decide with that token reports it. Either way the error carries its code and its one next step;
  - an adapter's connection set-up, inside its constructor, before the adapter accepts any call. The state lives only in the constructor's body. Before the constructor returns, built or failed, it copies the state's `reading()` and drops the state. The adapter keeps that copy, the `ClassReading` of the set-up's breaker, health reading, attempt counter and last code with the instant it was taken, and a failed construction returns it with its error. The adapter's health shows the copy only as the set-up's result, labelled as set-up with its instant, and never as the port's current breaker or health. No later call of the adapter can reach a state, because none is left.

  No instance that a request handler reaches builds a state: the ports of the embedded single-organisation server, the endpoint's local stores and the spool return their errors to their callers unretried until #1959 (R11). A state and its classes are built with their instance, never per call. The state holds nothing per caller: no key, count or history of who called (P8; RFC 0007 section 7).
- R10. **Derives.** Every public type here implements `Debug`, and each derives what sections 3 to 5 write beside it, so a test can unwrap a kit result and compare an error. `RetryState` and `ManualClock` are not `Clone`, because a copy would split one instance's state, or one test's time, in two. `RetryState`'s `Debug` prints no entropy, clock or health call. `Outcome` is not `Copy`, so RFC 0002's `NotApplicable(Fault)` can join it.
- R11. **Exactly two callers.** The executor's callers are the two of R9, and no call made for a request from outside the program runs through it until #1959 is decided (SR-new-0008-I, section 8). A request from a local client counts: `grdr join` and the Decide whose STEP_UP sends an ApprovalRequest are requests to the daemon, so the uplink calls they start are sent once, outside the executor, even when the call outlives the request (R9).
  - A caller uses `run` with no deadline, or `run_until` with a deadline computed from the state's clock and a fixed `limits.toml` row, never from a request. Each entry of `architecture.toml`'s list of `run_until` callers names the row its deadline reads.
  - Uplink code that may drop a run uses a class of its own.
  - An adapter's constructor is called only from a start-up module of a program's composition root: the program's start, and the module that applies a new settings revision (design/20 section 16.1) from the program's own task. A request that changes a setting stores the revision and returns, so no request waits on a set-up and no reset reaches one.
  - The lint holds the rule (section 8). `RetryState::new`, `for_class`, `run` and `run_until` appear only inside the body of a listed constructor, or in a listed module of `grdr-uplink` that makes the uplink's own calls; the modules that send the calls a local request starts are not listed. A listed constructor is called only from a listed start-up module. No struct of a layer 3 crate holds a `RetryState`. In a listed constructor's module, any other function that takes an operation or returns a `RetryState` is a wrapper and fails, and so does any other function that calls the constructor. Test code and the kit are exempt. The scan is a tripwire with review behind it (R9's row of section 7).
- R12. **Only the peer's failures count.** An operation's error type implements RFC 0007's `Coded`. A failed attempt counts against the peer when it ran to its cutoff without an answer, or when its error's code says the peer did not serve it. `faults_peer(class, connect)` is the one function that decides this, over the two fields of RFC 0007's registered code. It is true for 8 of the 80 pairings, when both of these hold:
  - the class is `retryable` or `fatal`, so the failure is neither the caller's (`input`, `policy`) nor a defect in the product's own code (`internal`);
  - the Connect code says the peer did not serve the call: `unavailable` (down), `deadline_exceeded` (the peer's own timeout, or a bound of ours that ended a call the peer never answered), `internal` (the peer's own error) or `unknown` (an answer nobody could name, which is how an unmapped transport error looks).

  The other twelve Connect codes are answers: `canceled` is our own cancellation; `invalid_argument`, `not_found`, `already_exists`, `out_of_range`, `failed_precondition` and `aborted` answer the request; `resource_exhausted` is back-pressure or a quota; `permission_denied` and `unauthenticated` are credentials, which a breaker cannot fix; `unimplemented` is a mismatch of versions; and `data_loss` reports one object the peer did serve, which is an alarm by its own code. Neither field alone is enough. One class, `retryable`, holds both a store that is down and a serialisation conflict. One Connect code, `internal`, holds both the peer's own error and a defect of ours.

  The cutoff is the class's call timeout, or a `run_until` deadline when it comes first, and a cut attempt only starts the health call, which decides whether the breaker opens. Counting only attempts that ran their full call timeout would let internal callers with a short deadline call a hanging peer without limit: about 5,900 calls in a minute in E2 b, against about 155. Counting none of the peer's own timeouts gave 6,606 calls with no opening in eval-adversarial's round 4 model; counting them gives 113 in this bench (C in section 7).

  The breaker treats the peer's answer as neither a failure nor a success: it records nothing. Recording it as a success would let cheap errors discard the failures of the calls in flight beside them, and would count an error the adapter decided on its own as the peer's answer. The state retries the answer under the policy, and the class counter adds one, as design/22 section 2.7 asks of the uplink. A class whose calls keep failing, with any codes, reaches its cap: `at_cap()` says so. A class whose calls mostly fail between successes never reaches it, so `failure_share()` gives the failed share of its attempts in its budget window. The uplink raises Degraded with `last_code()` while `at_cap()` holds or the share is above the `uplink.degraded-failure-share` row, and a cutoff's code is `GRDR-RETRY-0001`, so Degraded always carries a code (section 4).
- R13. **The health call stands for the calls.** Each class has its own health call, and it follows the route, the credentials and the verification of that class's calls. It waits on no connection, handle or slot that a call can hold or wait for. The adapter makes its connection again after an unhealthy verdict, so a stale connection of its own does not keep it failing. The health call is healthy only when it answers `Ok`: any error, counted or not, and no answer are unhealthy. Its verdict and code show in health. An adapter's health call fails when the adapter's calls fail: the kit's admission case induces a fault that fails every connect, reads the set-up reading the failed construction returns, and refuses a health call that stays healthy, as `async { Ok(()) }` does (section 7). The two ways a health call and the calls can disagree each have a bounded exit, stated in section 6.

### 3. Clock, Entropy and Monotonic (#281)
```rust
// grdr_model
#[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, PartialEq, Eq, PartialOrd, Ord, Hash)]
pub struct Monotonic { /* private: nanoseconds */ }
impl Monotonic {
    pub fn from_nanos(nanos: u64) -> Monotonic;
    pub fn checked_add(self, by: Duration) -> Option<Monotonic>;
    pub fn saturating_duration_since(self, earlier: Monotonic) -> Duration;
}

// grdr_ports::clock
pub trait Clock: Send + Sync {
    fn now(&self) -> Timestamp;
    fn monotonic(&self) -> Monotonic;
    fn sleep_until(&self, deadline: Monotonic) -> impl Future<Output = ()> + Send;
}
// grdr_ports::entropy
pub use grdr_model::Entropy;

// grdr_ports_testkit::clock
#[derive(Debug)]
pub struct ManualClock { /* private; Sync */ }
impl ManualClock {
    pub fn new(now: Timestamp, monotonic: Monotonic) -> ManualClock;
    pub fn advance(&self, by: Duration) -> Result<(), ClockError>;
    pub fn jump_wall(&self, by_ms: i64) -> Result<(), ClockError>;
    pub fn sleeps(&self) -> Vec<Monotonic>;
}
#[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, PartialEq, Eq)]
pub enum ClockError { Overflow }
pub fn suite<A: Clock>() -> Suite<A>;
// grdr_ports_testkit::entropy
#[derive(Clone, Debug)]
pub struct SeededEntropy { /* private */ }
impl SeededEntropy { pub fn from_seed(seed: u64) -> SeededEntropy; }

// grdr_ports_testkit, the crate root
pub use clock::{ClockError, ManualClock};
pub use entropy::SeededEntropy;
pub fn block_on<F: Future>(future: F, max_polls: u32) -> Result<F::Output, Stalled>;
#[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, PartialEq, Eq)]
pub struct Stalled { pub polls: u32 }
```

`Timestamp` and `Entropy` are `grdr-model` types that #383 builds and #1850 states. Semantics:
- `Monotonic` orders instants within one process; its arithmetic never wraps or panics. `from_nanos` takes a count of nanoseconds from an origin the clock fixes for the process, and instants of two processes are never compared. Nothing here sends one, and the one value section 4 lets a program persist, the attempt counter, holds none.
- `now` is wall time and may jump. `monotonic` never decreases within a process and counts time the host spent suspended, so a lease holder that wakes after a sleep sees its deadlines as passed and renews or reacquires at once. The OS adapter meets this with the boot-time count: `CLOCK_BOOTTIME` on Linux, `mach_continuous_time` on macOS, and `QueryInterruptTimePrecise` on Windows, the same interrupt-time count as `QueryInterruptTime` read from the timer hardware instead of at the last clock tick (#1927 R2).
- `sleep_until` completes once `monotonic()` is at or past `deadline`; a past deadline completes on the first poll.
- `ManualClock::advance` moves both clocks; `jump_wall` moves only `now`. `sleep_until(d)` completes on its first poll and, if `d` is later than the current monotonic value, advances both clocks to `d`. A sleep records its deadline when first polled; `sleeps` returns the recorded deadlines in order. Overflow returns `ClockError::Overflow`.
- `SeededEntropy` is SplitMix64, so one seed gives the same bytes on every target; its output for seed 0 equals the reference `splitmix64.c`. Its state is no secret, since a report records the seed, so it derives `Clone` and `Debug`.
- `block_on` drives a future with a no-op waker and returns `Stalled` once `max_polls` polls have returned `Pending`.

Suite `clock::suite()`, for any `A: Clock`, has three cases, in this order, with the parameters of `params.rs` (R5):
- `monotonic_never_decreases`: 1,000 reads of `monotonic()`, each at or after the one before.
- `sleep_reaches_deadline`: `d` is `monotonic()` plus 10 ms; `block_on(sleep_until(d), 1_000)` completes, and `monotonic() >= d` after it.
- `past_deadline_first_poll`: `d` is `monotonic()`; `block_on(sleep_until(d), 1)` completes.

Each case drives its future with `block_on`, so a clock runs the suite when its sleep completes by being polled. `ManualClock`'s does. An OS clock whose sleep waits on its runtime's timer runs the suite with a test timer that blocks the thread and is ready on its first poll: #1927's `OsClock<T: Timer>` takes its timer as a parameter for this, so the suite runs it as written (lead-core, #1924 comment 7075943320). The 10 ms offset keeps that run short. Callers of `Clock`: every target, the daemon, the executor.

`RetryPolicy`, `RetryStep`, `RetryBudget`, `Breaker`, `AttemptCounter` and the give-up reason `GiveUp` are `grdr-model` types that #282 builds and #1850 states (RFC 0007 section 1). `Code`, `Coded`, `ErrorClass` and `ConnectCode` are RFC 0007's. Section 8 lists what this section needs of #1850. `RetryPolicy::from_limits` reads RFC 0007's `RetryLimits`. The executor's code is `GRDR-RETRY-0001` `ATTEMPT_CUT`, `retryable` with `deadline_exceeded`, in the area `RETRY` of subsystem `grdr-ports`; `faults_peer` is true for it. The kit's codes, one class and Connect code each:
- `GRDR-KIT-0001` `SCRIPTED_UNAVAILABLE`: `retryable` with `unavailable`;
- `GRDR-KIT-0002` `SCRIPTED_PEER_DEADLINE`: `retryable` with `deadline_exceeded`;
- `GRDR-KIT-0003` `SCRIPTED_WATCHDOG`: `fatal` with `deadline_exceeded`;
- `GRDR-KIT-0004` `SCRIPTED_CONFLICT`: `retryable` with `aborted`;
- `GRDR-KIT-0005` `SCRIPTED_OVER_LIMIT`: `policy` with `resource_exhausted`;
- `GRDR-KIT-0006` `SCRIPTED_INPUT`: `input` with `invalid_argument`;
- `GRDR-KIT-0007` `SCRIPTED_DEFECT`: `internal` with `internal`.

`faults_peer` is true for the first three only. `Scripted::Unhealthy(f)` is an unhealthy health call for every `f`, because only `Ok` is healthy (R13).

Who calls (R9, R11):
- The outbound calls the uplink's own tasks start use `run`, or `run_until` with a deadline from a fixed `limits.toml` row. The executor never calls past that deadline, except that a run that makes the health call waits for that call, at most the class's call timeout. The calls a local request starts are sent once, outside the executor.
- An adapter's connection set-up uses `run` around its connect, inside the constructor that builds the adapter. The constructor copies `reading()` and drops the state before it returns. The adapter's later calls do not go through the executor.

The state:
- `RetryState::new(policy, clock, entropy, health)` builds the state and its first class (R9). It owns its policies, so a state never runs under a policy it was not built from. It owns its clock and its entropy, so no caller brings a skewed clock or an entropy that returns zeros into the state. Each class owns its health call. The executor borrows the state shared, so any number of concurrent calls use one state. It is `Send + Sync` and not `Clone`.
- `for_class(policy, health)` builds another operation class of the same instance, with its own health call on its own route. It shares the clock and entropy, and keeps its own policy, budget from the policy's budget fields, attempt counter and breaker, so one class's retries never spend another's. `set_policy(policy)` replaces the class's policy, keeping its breaker and every counter. It keeps the class's budget too, unless the policy's budget fields differ, when the class's budget starts again from empty under the new fields. A new revision of a server-set value, such as design/22's `Schedule` caps, therefore reaches a live state without resetting it.
- The class policies, budgets, counters and breakers, the abandoned count and the health readings sit behind one `std::sync::Mutex`. The executor holds the lock only while it reads or updates them, never across an `.await` and never while the operation or the health call runs. The draw from the state's entropy happens under it. A poisoned lock is taken back with `PoisonError::into_inner`, because no step that holds it runs caller code or a panic path, and each step leaves the state whole. The state reads no clock except its own, and every instant it records is one read from that clock.
- The attempt counter is design/22 section 2.7's, one per class. Every failed attempt of the class adds one, saturating, whatever the breaker records, and only the class's `Done` sets it to 0. The executor passes its value before the failure being recorded to `RetryPolicy::next`, as the `AttemptCounter` whose ceiling the delay is drawn under, and passes the run's own retries so far, a `u32`, for the give-up checks. So a new run continues the backoff the class reached, a table's `max_attempts` and `max_elapsed` still bound one call, and the two counts cannot be swapped.
- `attempt_counter()` reads the counter, and `resume(counter)` sets it on a newly built class, so a restarted program continues from it. design/22 section 2.7 keeps the uplink's counters in the daemon's state directory; #1238 (22-5, `uplink-reconnect`) writes them there and reads them back. Only the counters persist. A new process starts with an empty budget and a closed breaker, because both hold `Monotonic` instants, and with no last code.
- `breaker()` returns a copy of the class's breaker, and `abandoned()` the number of runs dropped before they returned. `health()` returns the class's last health reading: its instant, its answer with the code of a failed health call, and `refuted`, the confirmations the health call found healthy since the class's last call success; `healthy()` reads the answer. `at_cap()` says the class's counter has grown until its delay ceiling is the cap, so its calls keep failing whatever their codes. `failure_share()` is the failed share of the class's attempts in its budget window, in permille, and 0 while the window holds fewer than `breaker_failures` failed attempts, so one transient failure in a quiet window does not count. `last_code()` is the code of the class's last failed attempt in this process, `GRDR-RETRY-0001` for an attempt cut at its cutoff, and is kept across successes; it is none only before the class's first failure. `reading()` copies all of these for one class, with the instant of the copy, as a `ClassReading`.
- The uplink shows each class's open breaker, the abandoned count and the health reading in its health. It raises Degraded with the reading's code while `refuted` is above 0, and with `last_code()` while `at_cap()` holds or `failure_share()` is above the `uplink.degraded-failure-share` row. With no last code it raises neither, so a counter resumed at the cap raises Degraded only at the class's first failure in this process. No read changes anything, and health adds no probe of its own (P17).

The health call:
- `Health::check` is the class's own request to its peer. No caller's request shapes it: it carries no caller's data and its cost is fixed. It follows R13. Examples are `SELECT 1` on a connection the adapter's set-up opens for it, a HEAD of a health object of the bucket over the same endpoint and credentials, and, for each uplink class, a request design/22 already has on that class's route that changes nothing on the server, healthy only on a verified response (#1238).
- It runs to its own cutoff, the class's call timeout: a probe's health call to the `probe_until` its permit recorded, and a confirming one to its `check_until`, so no second clock read moves the cutoff. It is healthy only when it answers `Ok`.

A call:
- If a `run_until` deadline has passed, the run returns `Timeout` and makes no call.
- Before each call the executor reads its clock once. Under one hold of the lock:
  - If the class's breaker would refuse a permit at that instant, the run ends with `BreakerOpen { until, last }`, and nothing is recorded.
  - If the breaker is `HalfOpen` and that instant is at or after its `probe_until`, the probe in flight has no verdict, and it counts as no answer. A health probe adds one unhealthy probe; a call probe counts as a cutoff, which ends its turn. The step is drawn with the class's counter, which adds one, the breaker opens again for the step's `open_for`, and the run ends with `BreakerOpen { until, last }`.
  - If the permit would be a probe, the run makes the class's health call instead of its own call (below), and spends no budget. After `breaker_failures` unhealthy probe verdicts in a row, the probe is the run's own call instead, a call probe, which also spends no budget.
  - Otherwise it records a request in the class's budget for the first call, or spends from that budget for a later one. A budget refusal ends the run with `BudgetExhausted` with the breaker untouched.
- That instant is the call's `start`. The call races the operation against `clock.sleep_until(min(start + timeout, deadline))`, where `timeout` is the class's call timeout and `deadline` exists only for `run_until`. Each poll polls the operation first, so an operation ready on the same poll wins. If the sleep wins, the operation is dropped and the attempt counts as timed out: retryable with no hint, run to its cutoff. When the deadline ended it, or no retry follows, the run returns `Timeout`.
- On `Done(t)` the class's breaker records a success at the instant of the report, the class's counter goes to 0, and the run returns `Ok(t)`. The operation returns `Done` only for an answer it accepts as the peer's own; for the uplink that is a verified response (design/22 section 2.7).
- On `Retry`, `Fail` or a timeout, the executor reads its clock once. That instant is the breaker's record, the start of the sleep and the end of `elapsed`, which runs from the first call's instant. The executor asks the policy once for the next step, with the counter, the run's retries and no hint for `Fail` or a timeout, the class's counter adds one, and the class's budget records a failed attempt. The class's breaker records the failure only when it is a peer failure (R12) and no call of the class succeeded while it was in flight. If that recorded failure reaches `breaker_failures`, the run makes the class's health call before it goes on.
- `Fail(e)` returns `Failed(e)`. Otherwise the run sleeps until `now + delay` and calls again, or returns `GaveUp`, or `Timeout` if the last attempt timed out. A retry due at or after a `run_until` deadline is not made, and the run returns `Timeout`.

The breaker, one per class, whose states #1850 change 5 lists:
- `Closed` counts consecutive recorded failures. A success sets the count to 0. At `breaker_failures`, and outside the quiet period below, it moves to `Checking`. The run that recorded the last failure makes the health call, and every other call still passes meanwhile.
- From `Checking`, a healthy verdict or any call's success closes the breaker again, with the count at 0 and a quiet period of one class call timeout. No check starts during the quiet period, though failures still count. An unhealthy verdict opens it. A recorded failure while `Checking` changes nothing until `check_until`; a recorded failure at or after it starts a new check with a new `check_until`, so a check whose run was forgotten or never polled again still ends.
- `Open { until, .. }` refuses every permit of the class before `until`. `until` is the failing run's `open_for`: the smaller of its step's delay and the counter's ceiling, and at most the class's cap. A hint therefore delays only that run's own retry, and the class's peer is probed at least once per cap. Another class's breaker is not touched. `Open` carries the unhealthy probes in a row and, in a call-probe turn, the instant the turn ends.
- At or after `until`, the first permit is a probe. The breaker moves to `HalfOpen { probe_until, probe, .. }`, where `probe` says whether the probe is the health call or a call probe, and the class's other permits are refused until `probe_until`, the permit's instant plus the class's call timeout. A healthy verdict closes the breaker with the count at 0 and the same quiet period as from `Checking`, and a probing run that made the health call then makes its own call. An unhealthy health probe opens it again for the probing run's `open_for` and adds one to the unhealthy probes: its step is drawn with its class's counter, which adds one, and the run returns `BreakerOpen`.
- The call probes after `breaker_failures` unhealthy health probes make a call-probe turn. The turn begins at its first call probe and lasts at most the class's cap. A call probe is timed by the class's own call timeout and closes the breaker only on `Done`. A peer failure or its cutoff ends the turn, opens the breaker again, and the next `breaker_failures` probes are health calls again; the run returns `BreakerOpen`. Any other error gives no verdict: the slot is freed at once, as `Open` with `until` at that instant, the next permit is a call probe again, and the run goes on with that error as section "A call" says. A permit after the turn's end is a health probe with the count at 0. During the turn, a run of the class that does not hold the call probe meets `BreakerOpen` with `until` at the call probe's `probe_until`.
- A probe that has no verdict at its `probe_until`, because its peer never answered, its timer fired late or its future is no longer polled, counts as no answer when the class's next permit is taken at or after that instant (section "A call"). The old verdict, if it ever comes, no longer applies. So every probe ends with one verdict, its own or the one the next permit records, whichever comes first, and however many callers wait on the class.
- So a health call that fails while the class's calls would succeed keeps the class's breaker open for at most `breaker_failures + 1` open periods of at most the class's cap each, plus their probes, with any number of callers (F3 in section 7), and a turn whose call probes all meet errors the peer did not cause ends within one cap.
- A late report comes from a call permitted while the breaker was closed. On `Open`, a late success makes the next permit a probe, so `Open` is still left only through `HalfOpen`, and a late failure leaves `until` as it was. On `HalfOpen`, a late success closes the breaker as a healthy verdict does, and a late failure changes nothing. A verdict applies only if the breaker is still in the state the check or probe started from.

Abandoned runs:
- Every run holds a drop guard. If its future is dropped before it returns, the guard adds one to `abandoned()`.
- If the dropped run was making a probe, the drop guard frees the slot, and the class's breaker is `Open` until one class call timeout from the drop. Another class's breaker is not touched.
- If the dropped run was making a confirming health call, the class's breaker returns to `Closed` with no verdict, its count one below `breaker_failures` and a quiet period of one class call timeout from the drop. The next recorded failure after the quiet period starts the check again.
- The drop guard reads only the state's own clock and calls no caller code.
- An ordinary call dropped with its run records no outcome. The budget keeps the request or retry it recorded before the call began.

Common to all:
- A deadline that would pass the maximum instant is the maximum instant, as the breaker's `until` is (#282).
- The race is written over `core::future::poll_fn`, with no dependency and no runtime. Every variant keeps the attempt count and the last error.
- The futures of `run` and `run_until` are `Send` under these bounds. A compile-time test shows it, and so also shows that no lock guard is held across an `.await`.

Callers: the outbound calls the uplink's own tasks start, and an adapter's connection set-up inside its constructor (ARCH-018 forbids any other retry loop), each through its state (R9).

### 5. Conformance runner (#281)
```rust
// grdr_ports_testkit::conformance
#[non_exhaustive]
pub struct Case<S> { pub name: &'static str, pub run: fn(&S, &mut SeededEntropy) -> Outcome }
#[derive(Clone, Copy, Debug, PartialEq, Eq)]
pub struct Failure { pub check: &'static str }
pub struct Suite<S> { /* private: ordered cases */ }
impl<S> Suite<S> { pub fn run(&self, subject: &S, adapter: &str, seed: u64) -> Report; }
#[non_exhaustive]
#[derive(Clone, Debug, PartialEq, Eq)]
pub enum Outcome { Pass, Fail(Failure) }
#[non_exhaustive]
#[derive(Clone, Debug, PartialEq, Eq)]
pub struct CaseResult { pub name: &'static str, pub outcome: Outcome }
#[derive(Clone, Debug, PartialEq, Eq)]
pub struct Report { /* private: kit version, adapter, seed, results */ }
impl Report {
    pub fn kit_version(&self) -> &str;
    pub fn adapter(&self) -> &str;
    pub fn seed(&self) -> u64;
    pub fn results(&self) -> &[CaseResult];
    pub fn passed(&self) -> bool;
    pub fn to_toml(&self) -> String;
}
// Case<S> is Clone, Copy and Debug, and Suite<S> is Debug, for every S.
```

Rules of the runner:
- This RFC holds the one definition of `Case`, `Suite`, `Outcome`, `CaseResult`, `Report` and the runner. RFC 0002 (#1688) amends them for the storage suites: `kind` on `Case` and `CaseResult`, `Outcome::NotApplicable(Fault)`, the `Fixture` bound on each storage suite's constructor, and the arming state `run` holds. #281 builds the runner as this RFC states it.
- A case returns its `Outcome`, so a storage case whose fixture cannot induce a fault returns `NotApplicable(fault)` once RFC 0002's amendment adds that variant. No side channel is needed.
- Only the kit builds a `Suite`, since it has no public constructor, so the cases a subject passes are the kit's own. The report's kit version is the `grdr-ports-testkit` crate version, and a change to any case is a new version. An adapter states the kit version it passed (design/03, extension points).
- `Report`'s fields are private and read through its accessors, so code outside the kit can read a report but cannot build one or edit one it holds. `Case`, `CaseResult` and `Outcome` are `#[non_exhaustive]`, so code outside the kit cannot build them, and a field or variant added by amendment breaks no caller: a match on `Outcome` outside the kit already needs a wildcard arm.
- `Suite::run` has no bound on `S`. A suite's constructor carries the bound its cases need, as `clock::suite<A: Clock>()` does, and `run` may hold state the kit keeps per process for the whole run. So neither RFC 0002's `Fixture` bound nor its arming changes `run`.
- `run` runs every case once, in order, on the calling thread. It builds one `SeededEntropy::from_seed(seed)` and passes it to each case in turn, so the same kit version, subject and seed replay the same run, and the report records the seed. A failing case does not stop the run.
- A `Failure` names the kit's own check and holds no text from the subject, so nothing an adapter returns, such as a driver error that echoes a connection string, reaches a report or a CI log.
- `passed()` is true when no case failed. `to_toml` renders the report as a TOML document and writes each string as a TOML basic string, with the quotation mark, the backslash and every control character escaped, so no adapter name adds a key or a line.

### 6. Failure modes
| Failure | Declared outcome | Visible as |
|---|---|---|
| A future does not complete under `block_on` | `Err(Stalled { polls })` once `max_polls` polls have returned `Pending`; it never spins without a bound | The caller's error |
| A subject fails a case | That case's outcome is `Fail` with the kit's check; the run goes on to the next case, and `passed()` is false | The report: case, adapter, seed and kit version |
| A subject panics inside a case | The test fails on the panic and no report is produced, so the run never passes | The test harness's output |
| `ManualClock` arithmetic would overflow | `advance` and `jump_wall` return `ClockError::Overflow` and leave both clocks unchanged | The returned error |
| `Monotonic` arithmetic would overflow | `checked_add` returns `None`; `saturating_duration_since` given a later instant returns zero | The returned value |
| A deadline of the executor would pass the maximum instant | It is the maximum instant | The deadline in `ManualClock::sleeps()` |
| A call reaches a port from a request handler: the embedded server's ports, the endpoint's local stores, the spool, or any adapter after its set-up | No `RetryState` serves it until #1959 is decided: the error goes back to the caller unretried, and a client that resets the request reaches no breaker (SR-new-0008-I) | The caller's error |
| A local request starts an uplink call: Enrol for `grdr join`, or a STEP_UP's ApprovalRequest to a remote approver | Sent once, outside the executor; its error goes back to the request, and a reset or a slow request reaches no breaker of the uplink's classes (R9, SR-new-0008-I) | The request's error, with its code and next step |
| An adapter's back end is down when the adapter is built | The set-up retries the connection under its policy and the class's breaker. When the policy gives up or the breaker opens, construction fails with the error's code and the set-up reading, and the adapter accepts no call. The state is dropped either way | The composition root's error, with the set-up reading |
| An adapter's back end fails after the adapter is built | Every call returns its error unretried (R9). The set-up reading does not change, and health never shows it as the port's current breaker or health | The callers' errors; the adapter's own health; the set-up reading, labelled with its instant |
| The peer never answers | Each attempt is dropped at its cutoff and counts as a peer failure. After `breaker_failures` of them the health call confirms and the breaker opens; each open period ends with one probe | `BreakerOpen` to refused callers; the open breaker in health |
| The peer fails every call and callers keep starting runs | Once a class's breaker has opened, the class reaches the peer with one probe per open period, and every other call of the class ends with `BreakerOpen` and no call. Each class's counter grows across runs up to its cap (R8) | `BreakerOpen`; the open breaker in health |
| One class's route fails while another's works | Only the failing class's breaker opens; the other class's calls go on (F2 in section 7) | That class's open breaker in health |
| One class's calls are retried often with errors that are not the peer's, such as conflicts | Its retries spend its own budget only, so another class's retries go on (A4 in section 7) | That class's `BudgetExhausted` |
| A call fails for its own reasons: input, over a quota, a conflict | Not a peer failure (R12): the breaker records nothing. The run retries it under the policy, and the class counter adds one (design/22) | The caller's error if the run gives up |
| A class's calls keep failing with codes `faults_peer` does not count, such as `permission_denied` | The breaker stays closed and makes no health call. The counter grows to the cap, so the class backs off to its cap, and `at_cap()` holds with `last_code()` naming the code (D in section 7) | Degraded with `last_code()` while `at_cap()` holds |
| A class's calls mostly fail but some succeed, so its counter never reaches the cap, such as a proxy that passes one call in seven | `failure_share()` over the class's budget window is above the `uplink.degraded-failure-share` row (A3 in section 7). A transient failure in a quiet window does not count (A5) | Degraded with `last_code()` while the share is above the row |
| Every attempt of a class is cut at its cutoff, against a peer that hangs | `last_code()` is `GRDR-RETRY-0001`, so Degraded at the cap carries it (A1 in section 7) | Degraded with `GRDR-RETRY-0001` |
| A counter resumed at the cap when the program starts | No Degraded before the class's first failure in this process, which sets `last_code()`; the counter still times the backoff | Degraded with that failure's code, from the first failure |
| A call times out while the peer serves other calls of its class | Not recorded, because the peer served a call meanwhile; if nothing else succeeded, it is recorded, and a healthy health call keeps the breaker closed | One health call at most per class call timeout |

| An adapter registers an error that is not the peer's with a peer-failure code | The breaker records it, and the health call still finds the peer healthy, so the breaker stays closed | `refuted` in health; the codes case that walks the registry (section 7) |
| The health call passes while every call fails | The breaker stays closed. At most one health call per health latency plus quiet period. | The health call fails while the calls would succeed, for example a filter on its path or a stale connection of its own | It decides only after recorded failures, so a broken health call opens the breaker only when calls also fail. Once open, the adapter makes the health call's connection again after each unhealthy verdict, and after `breaker_failures` unhealthy probes in a row the next probes are call probes, timed by the class's own call timeout, which close the breaker on `Done`: at most `breaker_failures + 1` open periods of at most the class's cap, plus their probes (P6d in section 7) | The health reading's code in health, against the call probe's success |
| A call probe meets an error the peer did not cause | No verdict: the slot is freed, the next permit is a call probe again, and the run goes on under R12. | A run of the class starts while a call probe is in flight | `BreakerOpen` with `until` at the call probe's `probe_until`: the run makes no call (R1 in section 7) | `BreakerOpen` |
| A health call answers an error, counted or not | Unhealthy (R13), so a dead peer whose health call answers `input` or a defect of ours still opens the breaker (H1 in section 7) | The reading's code in health |
| A health call waits on a connection or slot that calls hold | R13 forbids it: the adapter keeps a connection, handle or slot for the health call alone, and its item's test holds every other one with paused calls while the health call completes | The adapter item's test |
| Internal code drops a run during a probe | The slot is freed, and the class's breaker stays open for one class call timeout from the drop; other classes' breakers are not touched; uplink code that shares a class with others makes them wait for that time | `abandoned()` in health |
| Internal code drops runs without passing a deadline, against a peer that hangs | Each dropped attempt records nothing, so the breaker stays closed. R11 asks such code to spawn the call, use its own class or pass `run_until`'s deadline | `abandoned()` grows with the calls |

| A live health call reports after its deadline | The verdict applies only if the breaker is still in the state it started from | The breaker's state |
| A call permitted while the breaker was closed reports after it opened | On `Open`, a success makes the next permit a probe and a failure leaves `until` unchanged; on `HalfOpen`, a success closes the breaker and a failure changes nothing | The breaker's state |

| `set_policy` is given a table whose budget fields differ | The class's budget starts again from empty under the new fields; its breaker and counter are kept | The class's next retries |
| The budget is spent | `BudgetExhausted { attempts, last }`; the breaker is not touched | The returned error |
| The operation returns `Fail` | `Failed(e)` after that call; it is a peer failure only if its code says so | The returned error |
| The policy gives up after a `Retry` | `GaveUp { reason, attempts, last }` | The returned error |
| A resumed attempt counter is stale or altered | Any value is a valid counter; the delay stays within the table's cap | The delays of the next run |
| The state's lock is poisoned | Taken back with `into_inner`; no step that holds the lock runs caller code or a panic path, so the state is whole | Nothing |
| The wall clock jumps during a run | No effect: elapsed time and deadlines use `monotonic()` only | #283's case with `jump_wall` between attempts |
| The host suspends during a run | A deadline that passed while the host was suspended has passed on waking, so the next attempt or the timeout comes within `clock.recheck` of waking (#1927 R8) | #1927's sleep model; #1936 on real cells |
| The system generator fails | The programs' `Entropy` never returns bytes it did not draw from the generator: zeroed or repeated bytes would make every full-jitter delay 0, and every key and nonce predictable. How the failure reaches the executor is #1850's; question 2 gives what each option does to `RetryError` | #1850's error, or the give-up reason the run returns |
| A caller meets a `RetryError` | It maps the variant to its own subsystem's code, and its rule class decides the outcome (D-15). A caller refused with `BreakerOpen` that can wait retries once at `until`, which takes the next probe, or counts the probe in flight as no answer | The caller's error and code |

Nothing here fails open: no `RetryError` variant reads as success, a variant a caller does not map fails its build, and none of #1850's options for a failed draw returns bytes that were not drawn. A failure that does not count against the peer is still returned to its caller when the run gives up.

### 8. Stability, and what each item changes
Stability:
- Both crates enter `surfaces.toml` as `experimental` (D-60; ADR-0020; design/03 section 7: every surface starts there), through the protected-change item filed on acceptance. Each gets one row of the kind `surfaces.toml` has when the item lands; in RFC 0005 R6.1's `[[crate]]` kind that is `name`, `class = "experimental"` and `rfc = "0008"`. RFC 0002 amends this RFC for both crates and keeps the rows. `Monotonic` takes the class of the `grdr-model` row (#1850), because a class belongs to a crate (RFC 0007 section 11).
- `experimental` allows a break in any release, and every change still goes by RFC. A signature, a semantic, a variant or a suite case is an amendment: a new RFC that names RFC 0008, because an accepted RFC never returns to `Review` (RFC 0001, "RFC states"). Its pull requests name that RFC for `rfc-required`. `cargo public-api` and `cargo semver-checks` run against the class once the rows exist, and the release notes list every amendment, as `breaking` when it breaks. #1959's decision is such an amendment.

Proposed for the protected change filed on acceptance:
- in `architecture.toml`: an empty third-party allow-list for each crate, with the rule that `grdr-ports-testkit` is in no layer 4 program's normal closure under any of that program's features, for each target triple of the support matrix;
- in ARCH-003's reader entries, the places `impl Clock` may appear outside test code (`grdr-os-clock` and `grdr-ports-testkit`) and those `impl Entropy` may (`grdr-crypto`'s `random` module and `grdr-ports-testkit`);
- the four lists ARCH-018's clause reads (R11), each an array of tables in the form `[[arch004.decoder]]` already has. Each entry names its crates by layer or by name, so an adapter or uplink item that follows the path conventions needs no protected change of its own; one that departs from them does, and its review sees why:
  - `[[arch018.retry_constructor]]` with `layer`, `module` and `function`: the constructors inside whose body the four calls may appear. One entry, `layer = 3`, `module = "src/setup.rs"`, `function = "connect"`, covers every adapter crate;
  - `[[arch018.retry_module]]` with `crate` and `modules`: the modules where they may appear anywhere. One entry, `crate = "grdr-uplink"`, `modules = "src/origin/*.rs"`, the modules #1238 writes for the calls the uplink's own tasks start. Enrol and the ApprovalRequest sit outside `src/origin/`;
  - `[[arch018.constructor_caller]]` with `layer` and `modules`: the start-up modules that may call a listed constructor. One entry, `layer = 4`, `modules = ["src/start.rs", "src/settings_loader.rs"]`, each program's start and the module that applies a settings revision from the program's own task;

It starts empty, and the item that first calls `run_until` in a module gets that module's entry through a protected change, so each deadline row is reviewed once;
- ARCH-017 exemption entries for `grdr_ports::retry::RetryError` (each caller maps every variant to its own code), and for `grdr_ports_testkit::clock::ClockError` and `grdr_ports_testkit::Stalled` (test kit errors, never returned by product code), and the entry that confines `codes::KIT_*` to `grdr-ports-testkit` and test code;

The entries take the form of the lists in force when the item lands (RFC 0002 section 14; RFC 0005 R4.1).

The `limits.toml` row `uplink.degraded-failure-share`, in `permille`, 500 by default, scope `fixed` and effect `none`, owned by design/22, is #1238's: its write set names `limits.toml` and it lands on the full lane, as RFC 0007 section 6 has every row (R5, R12). #1238's lead sets its `min` and `max` within 1 to 999, so no value turns the signal off.

Proposed for the design documents, each filed on acceptance as the chief architect's design item:

The clause matches resolved paths where the scan can, and #1582 names these bypass forms as constraints, each with a fixture that fails: a renaming `use`, a `type` alias, a fully qualified path, `Self::new` inside an impl, and, in a listed constructor's module, any other function that takes an operation, returns a `RetryState` or calls the constructor. It is a tripwire with review behind it.
- **design/03 section 10 (Retries) and design/22 section 2.7, the jitter.** The full jitter is drawn from the program's system generator (R1), not "seeded by device id, boot id and attempt". Independent draws from each device's generator keep devices out of step, which is what the seed was for. A seed built from those three values would let anyone who knows them predict a device's retries.
- **design/03 section 10 (Retries).** One sentence: until #1959 is decided the executor serves the calls the uplink's own tasks start and an adapter's connection set-up inside its constructor, a deadline comes from a fixed `limits.toml` row, and every other caller, a local request's uplink call included, returns its errors unretried. - **"After #1959" marks on the call-time rules.** Until #1959 is decided, these sentences bind an adapter's set-up and the uplink's own calls only, and each gains the mark: design/03 section 10's Recovery row ("every external call goes through a retry policy, so it has a timeout and a circuit breaker"), design/11 R18 ("every external call has a timeout, a named retry policy and a circuit breaker") and its section 12 bullet "Timeouts and circuit breakers" ("each destination has a circuit breaker that opens when the policy's failure budget is spent"), and design/20 section 1's Stability paragraph ("Every external call this document names ... has a timeout, a named retry policy ... and a circuit breaker"). - **design/22 section 2.7, the origin's breakers and budgets.** The paragraph already makes the `uplink-reconnect` policy "one instance per origin and operation class", and design/03 section 10 has each policy name its circuit breaker; only the breaker sentence and the budget sentence say otherwise, so the change settles design/22 with itself.
  - "Each origin has a circuit breaker that opens after the consecutive-failure count in `limits.toml` and closes on a successful probe at the backoff time, so a failing origin costs one probe per backoff while update fetches move to the next `updates.sources` entry" becomes: "each operation class of an origin has its own circuit breaker and health call, on the class's own route; a breaker opens after the consecutive-failure count in `limits.toml` when the health call confirms it, closes on a successful probe at the backoff time, and opens for no longer than its class's cap, so the class reaches the origin within that cap plus two of its call timeouts of its recovery; a failing origin costs each class one probe per backoff, about 3,600 / (cap / 2) calls an hour, while update fetches move to the next `updates.sources` entry". With one breaker per origin, a class whose route failed held the other class back (F2 and P6d of round 6). The second timeout covers a probe's health call in flight when the origin recovers. The cost is R8's 86.8 calls an hour per device against a dead origin, against 58 to 71 with one shared breaker.
  - "Retries are budgeted at 10% of the uplink's requests in any minute plus one every 10 s" becomes: "retries are budgeted per operation class at 10% of that class's requests in any minute plus one every 10 s, so one class's retries never spend another's". With one budget per uplink, a data class retrying conflicts once a second refused 16.0% of the control retries that needed one, and at four a second 25.0% (eval-security, round 7); with a budget per class, none (A4).
  - A sentence for health: "a class at its backoff cap, or whose failed share of attempts in its budget window is above `uplink.degraded-failure-share`, raises Degraded with the code of its last failed attempt, `GRDR-RETRY-0001` for a cutoff; calls a local request starts, Enrol and the ApprovalRequest, are sent once and return their errors to the request".
- **design/02 section 3.8.** Eight security requirements, from eval-security's wording in rounds 1 to 7 (#1924 comments 7076101630, 7076812230, 7077581411, 7078608559, 7079650893, 7080275083 and 7080886893):
  - One `RetryState` per caller instance holds, for each class, the budget, the attempt counter and the breaker for every call; one class's retries never spend another's budget. A new run continues its class's counter, only a success resets it, and a program can persist and resume it.
  - A probe stops holding the breaker by its deadline, a probe with no verdict at its deadline counts as no answer at the next permit, an abandoned probe frees its slot at once, and only `Done` closes the breaker on a call probe.
  - Product code draws randomness only from the system generator. No deterministic `Entropy` is in a program's normal closure, and `Clock` and `Entropy` are implemented only where ARCH-003 names.
  - Only peer failures by their code and cutoffs count, and a class's breaker opens only when its health call also fails.
  - A run that internal code abandons is counted.
  - SR-new-0008-G, the health call stands for the calls: each class's health call follows the route, credentials and verification of that class's calls, waits on no resource a call can occupy, is healthy only on `Ok`, and its code shows in health. Test: a fault that fails the class's calls fails its health call too, a filter that fails only the health call is ended by the call probe, and a second class whose calls outlast the first class's call timeout ends its own (P6d).
  - SR-new-0008-I, no request reaches the executor: no call made for a request from outside the program runs through the executor until #1959 is decided, a local request's uplink call included, and a set-up state does not outlive its constructor. Test: the reset test of R11's row in section 7, on `grdr-server`, the daemon and `grdr-uplink`.
  - SR-new-0008-J, health says what failed: Degraded raised at a class's cap or above its failure-share row carries a registered code, `GRDR-RETRY-0001` after cutoffs, and an adapter's set-up reading is never shown as its current health. Tests: A1 and A3 of section 7, and an outage after construction that leaves no `Closed` breaker in the adapter's current health.

  Residual risks for its 3.9:
  - uplink code that cancels runs on a class it shares with other uplink code; E4b over 300 s shows the others refused 39% of the time;
  - internal code that drops runs instead of spawning them or passing `run_until`'s deadline;
  - a peer whose health call passes while its calls fail, bounded only by the budget and the counters and shown as Degraded;
  - a class whose calls fail in bursts below `breaker_failures` failed attempts per budget window, or below the failure-share row, raises no Degraded; its errors still reach their callers.

  The first two are visible in `abandoned()`. The requirements for every other caller, SR-new-0008-F among them, are #1959's.

## Alternatives
- **Keep sections 3 and 4 in RFC 0002.** #281 and #283 would wait on a tenant boundary they do not use. Rejected.
- **Keep the machinery for outside callers, many principals and the decide path here, as revisions 4 and 5 did.** Every remaining major of round 5 sat in it, and it needs design that design/07, design/20 and design/22 do not settle yet. Slice 0's first runnable target needs none of it. Rejected for #1959, with a boundary rule that keeps those ports off the executor until it is decided.
- **Keep the embedded server's ports and the endpoint's stores on the executor, as revision 6 did, with `Serves::One` to mark them.** Request handlers reach those instances, so outside clients could hold their breakers, and a declaration that every caller can make checks nothing (round 6). Rejected for exactly two callers that a lint names by constructor and module.
- **An adapter that keeps its set-up state to show its breaker and health, as revision 7 had it.** Nothing drove the state after set-up, so its reading never changed while the back end could die, and a call path in the constructor's module could still run through it (round 7). Rejected for a copied reading, labelled as set-up, and a state that ends with its constructor.
- **A lint list of constructor modules, as revision 7 had it.** A module is coarser than a call path, and the wrapper form it named described every constructor (round 7). Rejected for constructors listed by function, the modules that may call them, and no `RetryState` field in an adapter crate.
- **The uplink calls a local request starts on a class of their own.** It would keep their drops off the uplink's own classes, but would keep a retry loop inside a request's life, and nothing could check the class rule. Rejected for sending them once, outside the executor.
- **A probe with no verdict superseded by the next permit, as revisions 4 to 7 had it.** With more than one caller waiting on a class, the next permit at `probe_until` started a new probe and the old probe's no-answer never counted (F3, round 7). Rejected for counting it as no answer at that permit.
- **One retry budget per uplink, as design/22 section 2.7 has it.** One class's retries of errors that are not the peer's spent the other's (A4). Rejected for a budget per class.
- **Degraded only at the cap.** A proxy that passes one call in seven keeps the counter below its cap, and a cutoff had no code (A1, A3). Rejected for a failure share above a row and a code for a cutoff.
- **A failure share with no floor.** One transient failure in a quiet window reads 1,000 permille until its retry succeeds, so Degraded would flicker. Rejected for a share that counts once `breaker_failures` attempts in the window have failed.
- **One breaker per origin shared by its classes, as revisions 3 to 6 had it.** A call probe of the class with the longer call timeout was cut at the shorter one, and a failure on one class's route held the other class back, so the bound of `breaker_failures + 1` open periods did not hold (P6d and F2 of round 6). Timing each call probe by its own class instead leaves a long call probe holding the shorter class's calls for the long timeout. Rejected for a breaker and a health call per class, at the cost of R8's 86.8 calls an hour against a dead origin instead of 58 to 71.
- **A runner bound to a storage `Fixture`, as RFC 0002 section 5 had it.** The Clock suite has no store, so it could not run, and #281 would wait on the tenant types the `Fixture` names. Rejected for a runner generic over its subject.
- **Exhaustive runner types, a `Report` with public fields, and cases that return `Result<(), Failure>`.** A field or variant RFC 0002 adds would break every caller. Any holder could edit a report into a pass, and a case could not report `NotApplicable`. Rejected for `#[non_exhaustive]`, private fields and cases that return an `Outcome`.
- **A `Clock` over `std::time::Instant` in `grdr-ports`.** The standard library does not specify whether `Instant` counts time the host spent suspended, and says the behaviour varies by platform and release (https://doc.rust-lang.org/std/time/struct.Instant.html, read 2026-11-22). A deadline could stay in the future after a sleep, and layer 2 would read a clock. Rejected for OS adapters in the programs.
- **`async-trait` or a boxed future, for a dyn-compatible `Clock`.** A new dependency and an allocation per sleep, while each program names its clock type at compile time anyway. Rejected.
- **A state borrowed by one run at a time, with no counter, as revision 1 had it.** Against a dead peer, 1,000 callers in one minute with a state each make 7,000 calls, and one caller that restarts after each error makes about 1,460 an hour. Rejected for one shared state per instance.
- **The clock and entropy as arguments of each run, as revision 2 had them.** One caller's clock with a distant origin, or an entropy that returns zeros, would time the breaker every other caller shares. Rejected for the state owning both.
- **Counting an abandoned attempt as a failure.** An OS network change that cancels the uplink's calls would open its breaker and block design/22's immediate reconnect. Rejected for counting abandoned runs in `abandoned()`.
- **The operation choosing, per call, whether a failure is the peer's.** A wrong choice on one call would count it. Rejected for `faults_peer` over each error's registered code, which is reviewed once in the registry.
- **An error the peer did not cause recorded as a success on the breaker.** It would discard the failures of calls in flight beside it, so a stream of cheap input errors would keep a failing peer's breaker closed. Rejected for recording nothing (R12), and for a call probe that closes only on `Done`.
- **Counting a timeout only when the attempt ran its full call timeout.** Internal callers whose `run_until` deadline is shorter than the call timeout would never open the breaker against a hanging peer: E2 b gives about 5,900 calls in a minute with the breaker closed, against about 155. Rejected, because no request sets that deadline and the health call still decides.
- **`faults_peer` over the class alone, or the Connect code alone.** R12 names a pairing that each field alone gets wrong. Rejected for both fields.
- **Counting class `internal` as a peer failure, as revision 4 did.** It is a defect in the product's own code, and says nothing about the peer. Rejected.
- **A health call healthy on an error `faults_peer` does not count, as revision 5 had it.** A health call that answers `input`, or a defect of ours, would keep the breaker closed against a dead peer, and `Scripted::Unhealthy(f)` was healthy for four of its seven faults. Rejected for healthy only on `Ok`.
- **Trusting the health call for ever once the breaker is open.** A filter on the health call's path alone held the breaker open with no limit in round 4. Rejected for the call probe after `breaker_failures` unhealthy probes.
- **Opening the breaker after `breaker_failures` refuted confirmations.** It would let failures the peer did not cause open the breaker after all, which the health call exists to prevent. Rejected: a health call that passes while calls fail is bounded by the budget and the counters and shown as Degraded.
- **Spawning the health call from `grdr-ports`.** That needs an async runtime in layer 2. Rejected: the state's health call runs inside the run that needs it.
- **A `retry-bounds.hint-ceiling` row.** It would be a new bound to set and keep, while the counter's ceiling already bounds what the policy itself would wait. Rejected for `open_for`, which needs no row.
- **A call-probe turn with no time exit, as revision 6 had it.** A peer that answers every call with an error no call probe can judge kept the turn going, and every caller met `BreakerOpen`, until a `Done` came (round 6). Rejected for a turn of at most one cap.
- **A health signal only for counted failures.** A class whose calls all fail with `permission_denied` or another code `faults_peer` does not count never opens its breaker and made no health call, so its health showed nothing (round 6). Rejected for `at_cap()` and Degraded.
- **`RetryError` as `#[non_exhaustive]`.** Each caller maps every variant to its own code, so a wildcard arm would map a variant added later with no decision. Rejected.

## Drawbacks
- The runner changes shape when RFC 0002 adds case kinds and `NotApplicable`. `#[non_exhaustive]` keeps that from breaking callers outside the kit, but it is still a change inside it.
- `Clock` is not dyn-compatible, so every caller is generic over its clock, and `RetryState` is generic over its clock, entropy and health call.
- The Clock suite drives futures with `block_on`, so an OS clock runs it with a test timer that blocks, not with its runtime's timer.
- Until #1959 is decided, every port a request handler reaches retries nothing after its set-up and has no breaker of the executor's, on the multi-tenant server and the embedded one alike, and so do the endpoint's stores and the spool. Their callers see each error once.
- With a breaker per class, the uplink against a dead origin makes 86.8 calls an hour per device instead of 58 to 71, about 241,000 requests a second at ten million devices.
- With a budget per class, the 10% share is the same over the device's requests, but the free retry every 10 s applies to each class, so two classes may make two every 10 s instead of one. Each class stays within its own budget, and the breakers and counters still bound a dead origin.
- An adapter shows no live breaker after its set-up. Its current health comes from its own errors and watchdog; the set-up reading says only how the set-up went.
- A `grdr join` or a STEP_UP's ApprovalRequest that meets a transient error fails at once, and the person or agent that started it tries again from the error's next step.
- Degraded from the failure share lags by up to one budget window and needs `breaker_failures` failed attempts in it, so a class that fails in short bursts may show nothing while its callers see the errors.
- Every class that calls a peer needs a health call that follows its calls' route on a connection of its own. Its cost is one call per confirmation and per open period, and a confirmation adds up to one class call timeout before a dead peer's breaker opens. In E2 that is about 211 calls in the first minute, against 118 in revision 3's bench.
- A run that makes the health call returns up to one class call timeout after its `run_until` deadline.
- After `breaker_failures` unhealthy probes in a row the probes are calls of the class's own, timed by its call timeout, for at most one cap. They come only while the health call is already failing, and other runs of the class meet `BreakerOpen` while one is in flight.
- A health call that passes while the calls fail keeps the breaker closed. The budget and the counters bound the load, and health shows it, but the class keeps calling: about 114 calls an hour per device on the uplink, against 87 for a dead origin.
- Uplink code that cancels runs on a class it shares with other uplink code can make that code wait at each probe: with every run dropped 1 ms in, the breaker stays open for most of 30 s, and over 300 s the others are refused 39% of the time. R11 asks such code to use its own class, and `abandoned()` shows it.
- Every call of an instance takes the state's lock two or three times. Each hold is short and never spans an `.await`, but a busy instance contends for it.
- The counter spans runs, so after an outage a class notices recovery only at its next probe, up to its cap after the last failure. design/22 section 2.7 asks for this.
- Readers find the slice-0 surface of `grdr-ports` in two RFCs, and the executor's other callers in a third.

## Unresolved questions
1. **#1850's signatures.** The give-up reason's name (`GiveUp` here; #1850 question 5) and the uses section 8 lists for #1850 are #1850's to state. This RFC can be decided first: #1850's AC-3 holds its types to the uses here, and a difference is settled by an amendment of this RFC, or by #1850's RFC while it is in review, naming both.
2. **A failed draw from the system generator.** The executor follows #1850's decision on `Entropy::fill` (lead-core, #1850 comment 7075943583). What each option does here:
   - Option 1, a fallible `fill`, with `RetryPolicy::next` turning a failed draw into a give-up with a reason of its own and the step's ceiling, with no jitter, as its `backoff` and `open_for`: the executor times the breaker by that ceiling and returns `GaveUp { reason, .. }`. `RetryError` does not change.
   - Option 1, with `next` returning the entropy error instead: the executor needs a new `RetryError` variant, by amendment of this RFC, and each caller maps it (R6).
   - Option 2, an infallible `fill` over a generator whose failure the adapter latches: `next` and `RetryError` do not change, and #1850 states how the adapter keeps section 6's rule that it never returns bytes it did not draw.

   This RFC prefers the first form of option 1. Its decision waits on #1850 only if #1850 chooses the second.

### Reasons
1. **Scope.** Splitting sections 3 and 4 out of RFC 0002 lets #281 and #283 build against a decided text while RFC 0002's tenant boundary is still in review (Motivation). Narrowing the executor to two callers, the calls the uplink's own tasks start and an adapter's connection set-up inside its constructor, removed every major of rounds 5 to 7 that sat in machinery slice 0 does not need. That machinery is #1959's (section 1).
2. **Layers.** Layers 0 and 1 read no clock or randomness and take `now` and an `Entropy` as arguments. Only `grdr-os-clock` and `grdr-crypto`'s `random` module read the system, and the kit's deterministic adapters reach no program (R1, R3, R4; D-05, P17).
3. **One executor, held by data and tests.** There is one retry loop, one state per instance and one budget, counter, breaker and health call per class. A failure counts against the peer only by its registered code or its cutoff, and a class's breaker opens only when its health call agrees (R9, R12, R13). ARCH-018's four lists and their fixtures hold who may call it, with review behind the scan (R11; P14).
4. **Bounds.** Against a dead origin each class reaches the peer about once per backoff, about 241,000 requests a second at ten million devices. That is the cost of a breaker per class against 161,000 to 197,000 with one shared breaker, which could not end a failure on one class's route. A probe with no verdict counts as no answer, so a class closes within `breaker_failures + 1` open periods however many callers wait (R8, section 4; P15).
5. **Visibility.** Degraded carries a code in every case it is raised, `GRDR-RETRY-0001` after a cutoff, and an adapter's set-up reading is never shown as its current health (R12, section 4; P9).
6. **Review.** Eight revisions answered every blocker and major. On `b338780` every required evaluator passed, `eval-architecture` on the other model from the metadata block, and no verdict on #1924 fails it. All nine seats support it with changes and none names a broken spine floor. Their changes and the round 8 minors are the constraints below, under the exit rule.
